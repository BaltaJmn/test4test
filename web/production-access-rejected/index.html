Production access rejected: what to change before you apply again

You applied for production access and the answer was not yes. Before you apply again, work out what was missing. This page sticks to what Google's help pages actually say.

What Google says can keep you in testing

Google's article says that if the app needs more testing, you may have to keep your closed test running. It gives two reasons: fewer than 12 opted-in testers, and insufficient tester engagement. It also says non-compliant apps lead to rejections, review delays and longer appeal processes, and that review is not a troubleshooting step.

The article does not describe a formal appeal process or a waiting period before you can apply again. If your rejection email gives details, follow those first.

"Testers were not engaged"

Google does not publish an exact measure in the article we checked. The wording points to testers using the app, not only installing it. So the safe reading is: your testers should open the app and try its features during the test.

What helps:

  • Tell testers up front that they must stay opted in for at least 14 days, as Google advises, and that you expect them to open the app.
  • Give each tester something to try: a feature, a flow, a setting.
  • Offer a feedback channel such as email or a group chat, and answer what comes in.
  • Check who is opening the app. Test4Test shows each owner on how many of the last 14 days every tester launched the app from Test4Test, so you can nudge the quiet ones. Opening the app is not the same as using its features, so ask testers to try them.

Fewer than 12 testers stayed opted in

Only testers who opted in and stayed opted in count. Someone who opts out before 14 days does not count, and if they come back, the 14 days must be consecutive. Count again in Play Console, add a few extra testers and let the clock run.

The app itself

Google tells developers to check policy compliance before applying. The article highlights four areas:

  • App content and features, including monetization.
  • App targeting and content rating accuracy.
  • Reliability: no crashes, broken functionality or missing screens.
  • Working test credentials for reviewers if the app requires login.

Keep fixing bugs and user-reported issues while the closed test runs, and look at the overall experience, not only crashes.

The answers in your application

The form asks how you recruited testers, whether they used all the features, whether their usage matched production behavior, and what feedback you got. It also asks what you changed because of the test and why you think the app is ready. Answer with facts you can back up. If testers did not use a feature, say so and say what you did about it.

Checklist before applying again

  1. At least 12 testers are opted in right now, and each has been opted in for the last 14 days without a gap.
  2. Testers have opened the app, not only installed it.
  3. You collected feedback and wrote it down.
  4. You fixed the problems it showed, and the app has no crashes or empty screens.
  5. If the app needs a login, reviewers have working credentials.
  6. Your answers in the form describe what really happened.

Keep the closed test running

Google says you may need to keep the test going, so do not shut it down after a rejection. Our reading, not Google's: testers who stay opted in should not need to start over. Use the time to fix what you found, ship a new build to the closed track and write down what changed, because the form asks for it.

Write the story down as you go

Keep a short log with three columns: what testers reported, what you changed and when. When you apply again, your answers come straight from that log and they match what really happened. It also tells you if one feature was never tested by anyone.

What not to do

  • Do not offer rewards for installs or reviews. Test4Test does not exchange reviews or ratings.
  • Do not apply again with nothing changed. Review is not a way to find out what is wrong.
  • Do not trust anyone who promises approval. Nobody can.
