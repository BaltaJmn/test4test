Need 12 testers for 14 days to pass Google Play closed testing? Test4Test is the tester community where Android developers test each other's apps and get production access.

Google Play asks every new personal developer account for a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days before it can publish. Finding those app testers is the part nobody tells you about, and Google turns down the production access request when testers do not really use the app.

Test4Test is test for test done properly: you list your app, other developers join your closed testing track, and you test theirs. Real people on real devices, not a bot farm and not a spreadsheet in a chat group.

WHAT YOU CAN DO

- List your app with its Google Group and its opt-in link from Play Console. That is all it takes.
- Browse the line of apps that still need testers, in order of arrival. Everyone pushes the first ones, so each app reaches 12 testers sooner.
- Join a closed test in four steps, in order: open the group, open the opt-in link, mark yourself in, install the app from Google Play.
- Leave a comment under an app to say you are in, ask a question, or link your own app so the developer can test you back.
- See who joined your test and whether they really have your app installed.
- Do your daily round: open the apps you test once a day from Test4Test. Google wants testers who use the app, not just install it, and the owner sees on how many of the last 14 days each tester opened it.
- Get a daily reminder while you have apps left to open, and a notification when a new tester joins your app. You can turn them off.
- Follow the 14-day streak: it starts the day you reach 12 testers, and the app tells you when it is done.

PROOF, NOT PROMISES

Pressing "I joined" proves nothing, and that is why most tester swaps fail. Test4Test checks on the tester's phone that your app is installed and that Google Play installed it, and shows it to you next to their name. It only looks at the apps they joined inside Test4Test, never at the rest of their phone.

HOW THE LINE WORKS

Apps wait in line in order of arrival, and the ones at the front get the testers until they reach 12; then they step aside and the next one moves up. Developers who test other apps go ahead of those who only post. Apps from subscribers go first and are marked as featured.

WHO IT IS FOR

Indie developers and small studios with a new Google Play Console account who need testers for closed testing, beta testers for a first release, or a way to keep 12 testers active for the full 14 days. It also helps if you already ran a closed test and Google rejected your production access: start again with testers who open the app every day.

FREE AND SUBSCRIPTION

The free plan lists one app at a time, which is what most developers need, and shows a small ad banner. The subscription puts your apps first in line, lists as many as you want and removes the ads; cancel it anytime from Google Play. Joining tests, commenting and browsing are always free.

PRIVACY

Your email address is never shown to anyone. Your name and profile picture are visible to other signed-in users, because that is how developers know who joined their test. Ads come from Google AdMob, and in Europe you choose whether they can be personalised. You can delete your account from the app at any time.
